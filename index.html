Brain-Inspired Computing & Embodied Intelligence

I am a PhD student at the College of Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence, Fudan University. My research lies in brain-inspired computing and embodied intelligence.

Much of my current work concerns efficient neural computation with spiking neural networks — models that exchange discrete events instead of dense values. I am interested in what such models can represent, how cheaply they can represent it, and where the inductive bias of spike-based computation actually helps. This line of work includes SpikeSTAG, which combines graph structural learning with spike-based temporal processing for multivariate time-series forecasting; SpikeLite, which keeps spiking forecasters lightweight through frequency-selective encoding and sparse cross-channel interaction; and QuantaSpike, which applies short-window spike-driven computation to quantized large language models.

More recently, I have been thinking about what comes after perception: memory. An embodied agent acting over a long horizon cannot retain everything it observes, so the question of what is worth keeping becomes as important as how observations are encoded in the first place.

Research Interests

Brain-Inspired Computing

I study spiking neural networks as a substrate for efficient computation, together with the representations that make them effective: temporal and spatio-temporal structure, spectral decomposition, and low-timestep encodings that compress information into few events.

This work is motivated by a simple question — how much of what a network computes is actually needed, and how much of the remainder can be removed before accuracy starts to matter.

Embodied Intelligence

I am interested in long-horizon memory for embodied agents: identifying the moments in a long interaction that remain useful later, retaining them in a compact form, and retrieving them when a decision depends on them.

I am also drawn to biological neural circuits, in particular those of the Drosophila brain, as a source of principles for organizing perception, memory, and action in small systems.

Selected Publications

  1. arXiv
    QuantaSpike: Short-Window Spike-Driven Quantization for Large Language Models
    Bang Hu, Guowei Zhu, Changze Lv, Xiaoqing Zheng, Fengzhe Zhang, Fan Zhang, and Wei Cao
    Sep 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    Large language models achieve strong performance across many tasks but rely on dense multiply-accumulate operations during inference, resulting in high energy cost. Spiking neural networks offer an event-driven alternative in which synaptic integration uses lightweight accumulation. However, spike-driven LLM inference remains difficult because outlier-heavy activations typically require long firing windows or auxiliary non-spiking paths. We propose QuantaSpike, a short-window spike-driven quantization framework for LLMs built around Logarithmic Ternary Integrate-and-Fire (LTIF) neurons.

  2. arXiv
    SpikeLite: Lightweight Spiking Neural Networks for Time-Series Forecasting
    Bang Hu, Changze Lv, Mingjie Li, Xiaoqing Zheng, Wei Cao, and Fan Zhang
    Sep 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    Spiking neural networks offer an energy-efficient paradigm for time-series forecasting through spike-driven computation. However, recent SNN forecasters often pursue higher accuracy through increasingly complex attention mechanisms or specialized neuronal dynamics, weakening the lightweight motivation of SNNs. We introduce SpikeLite, a spiking forecasting framework built around a Frequency-Selective Spiking Encoder (FSSE) and a Sparse Spiking Channel Attention (SSCA) module.

  3. arXiv
    SpikeSTAG: Spatial-Temporal Forecasting via GNN-SNN Collaboration
    Bang Hu, Changze Lv, Mingjie Li, Yunpeng Liu, Xiaoqing Zheng, Fengzhe Zhang, Wei Cao, and Fan Zhang
    Aug 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    Spiking neural networks, inspired by the spiking behavior of biological neurons, offer a distinctive approach for capturing the complexities of temporal data. However, their potential for spatial modeling in multivariate time-series forecasting remains largely unexplored. We introduce a spiking architecture that integrates graph structural learning with spike-based temporal processing, eliminating the need for predefined graph structures.

Current Research

Long-Horizon Memory for Embodied Agents. An agent interacting with the world over an extended period accumulates far more observation than it can store, and most of that observation is irrelevant to any future decision. I am interested in how such an agent should decide which moments to keep, how those moments should be represented so that they stay useful rather than merely present, and how the right memory is retrieved when a decision depends on it. Concretely, this covers keyframe detection and selection, event-aware memory representations, long-horizon visual memory, memory retrieval and utilization, and memory organizations that stay within a realistic budget.

This work is in progress. Results, papers, and systems will be listed here and on the publications page as they become available.
