research

Brain-inspired computing with spiking neural networks, and long-horizon memory for embodied agents.

My work follows a loose trajectory: efficient neural computation, then the representations that make that computation worthwhile, then the memory and behavior such representations can support. The two areas below describe where I currently spend most of my time.

Research Area 1 — Brain-Inspired Computing

Dense neural computation is expensive, and much of that expense is spent communicating values that carry little information. Spiking neural networks offer a different accounting: units exchange sparse, discrete events, and synaptic integration reduces largely to accumulation. That makes them a natural place to ask how much computation a task actually requires.

My work in this area is less about scaling spiking models up than about understanding what they are good at, and about keeping them efficient — in the number of timesteps, the amount of cross-channel communication, and the precision of the values they carry.

Representative work

SpikeLite: Lightweight Spiking Neural Networks for Time-Series Forecasting
Bang Hu, Changze Lv, Mingjie Li, Xiaoqing Zheng, Wei Cao, Fan Zhang.
Built around a frequency-selective spiking encoder and a sparse spiking channel attention module, with a lighter channel-independent path for cases where explicit channel interaction is not needed.
arXiv:2609.35097

QuantaSpike: Short-Window Spike-Driven Quantization for Large Language Models
Bang Hu, Guowei Zhu, Changze Lv, Xiaoqing Zheng, Fengzhe Zhang, Fan Zhang, Wei Cao.
Applies spike-driven computation to quantized LLMs through logarithmic ternary integrate-and-fire neurons, group-adaptive gain, and selective outlier admission, under a short firing window.
arXiv:2609.34259

SpikeSTAG: A Dendritic Compartmental Spiking Graph Network for Multivariate Time-Series Forecasting
Bang Hu, Changze Lv, Mingjie Li, Yunpeng Liu, Xiaoqing Zheng, Fengzhe Zhang, Wei Cao, Fan Zhang.
Integrates graph structural learning with spike-based temporal processing for multivariate time-series forecasting, without requiring a predefined graph.
Accepted at NeurIPS 2026.
arXiv:2508.02069

Research Area 2 — Embodied Intelligence

A. Long-Horizon Memory for Embodied Agents

An embodied agent operating over a long horizon observes far more than it can keep. Storage is not the only constraint: retaining everything makes retrieval harder, and most of what is retained will never matter again. The interesting problem is therefore selection — deciding what deserves to be remembered — together with the representation that lets a remembered moment stay useful.

Questions I am working on:

  • What makes a frame informative for future decisions?
  • How can important events be identified over long horizons?
  • How should an agent retrieve relevant visual memories, and what representation makes retrieval reliable?
  • How can memory be organized so that the cost of keeping it stays bounded as the horizon grows?

This covers keyframe detection and selection, event-aware memory representations, long-horizon visual memory, and memory retrieval and utilization.

B. Biological Inspiration from the Drosophila Brain

Biological nervous systems solve perception, memory, and action under constraints that no engineered system currently respects: very few neurons, very little energy, and no separation between remembering and acting. The Drosophila brain, whose circuitry is increasingly well mapped, is a useful scale at which to read those solutions off directly.

I am interested in what such circuits suggest for embodied intelligence — how sensorimotor processing is organized, how memory and action are coupled, and which of these principles transfer into models.

This is an exploratory interest rather than a completed line of work. I have no results, papers, or systems on biological circuit reconstruction to report yet.
